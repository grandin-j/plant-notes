## Planting Plan by Location

| Location | Replacing | Options |
|---|---|---|
| Frontyard — main bed | 9 Agave desmettiana | Agave parryi *or* Dasylirion longissimum |
| Frontyard — narrow planter at house & strip between driveway/entry walk | Juncus/sedge | Dianella tasmanica *or* Festuca ovina 'Elijah Blue' *or* Pennisetum setaceum 'Rubrum' |
| Backyard — bed | Bulbine | Tulbaghia violacea *or* Festuca ovina 'Elijah Blue' *or* Asparagus meyeri |
| Backyard — planters flanking bedroom sliding door (tall, upright) | — | Cordyline australis 'Atropurpurea' *or* Cordyline stricta *or* Cereus peruvianus |

## Agave parryi (Parry's Agave)

**Planned location:** Frontyard main bed, replacing the 9 existing Agave desmettiana (similar count/spacing, 1–5 gal).

![Agave parryi (Parry's Agave)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Agave%20parryi%20%28Jardin%20Majorelle%29.JPG?width=800)

A succulent plant with thick, gray-green leaves arranged in a rosette.

**South Oceanside performance:** Excellent fit. Extremely drought tolerant once established and well within its hardiness range in Oceanside's near-frost-free coastal climate (Sunset zone 24 / USDA ~10b). Its compact 2–3 ft rosette matches the scale of the agaves it's replacing. Needs full sun and fast drainage — South Oceanside's sandy loam suits it — and little irrigation beyond establishment.

[Photo source (Wikimedia Commons)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Agave_parryi_%28Jardin_Majorelle%29.JPG)

## Dasylirion longissimum (Mexican Grass Tree)

**Planned location:** Frontyard main bed, as the alternative to Agave parryi (fewer plants needed — about 4 at 5 gal — since each grows larger and fills more of the space the 9 agaves currently occupy).

![Dasylirion longissimum (Mexican Grass Tree)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Dasylirion%20longissimum%20-%20Jardin%20d%27oiseaux%20tropicaux%20-%20DSC04880.JPG?width=800)

Known for its symmetrical burst of long, slender green leaves.

**South Oceanside performance:** Very well suited to coastal San Diego — heat and drought tolerant, hardy well below Oceanside's coldest winter nights, and tolerant of the light salt-laden air common a few blocks from the beach. It grows slower than Agave parryi, so expect a couple of seasons before 4 plants fully read as a replacement for 9 agaves, but it needs even less ongoing upkeep long-term (no pups/offsets to remove).

[Photo source (Wikimedia Commons)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dasylirion_longissimum_-_Jardin_d%27oiseaux_tropicaux_-_DSC04880.JPG)

## Dianella tasmanica (Tasman Flax Lily)

**Planned location:** Frontyard narrow planter at the house and the strip between the driveway and entry walk, replacing the juncus/sedge.

![Dianella tasmanica (Tasman Flax Lily)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Dianella%20tasmanica%20-%20Berlin%20Botanical%20Garden%20-%20IMG%208746.JPG?width=800)

A perennial herb with strappy green leaves and blue berries.

**South Oceanside performance:** Good match for a narrow bed — its clumping, non-spreading habit stays tidy along a walkway. Prefers part shade/filtered sun and a bit more regular water than the succulent options nearby; Oceanside's mild, overcast-prone microclimate (marine layer) should keep the foliage from scorching, though a planter that gets strong west/afternoon sun off the house wall will need more irrigation than the plant's reputation suggests.

[Photo source (Wikimedia Commons)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dianella_tasmanica_-_Berlin_Botanical_Garden_-_IMG_8746.JPG)

## Festuca ovina 'Elijah Blue' (Blue Fescue)

**Planned location:** Offered as an option for *two* spots — the frontyard narrow planter/entry-walk strip, and the backyard bed replacing Bulbine.

![Festuca ovina 'Elijah Blue' (Blue Fescue)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Festuca%20glauca.jpg?width=800)

A dwarf perennial grass with powder blue, spiky leaves.

**South Oceanside performance:** Reliable low-water accent grass for either location. Its blue color is most vivid in full sun, and the area's mild, low-humidity coastal air actually favors it — inland heat is the more common cause of this grass browning out in summer, and Oceanside rarely gets hot enough for that to be an issue. As a cool-season bunchgrass it tends to die out in the center after 2–3 years and benefits from periodic division, so plan to refresh clumps in both beds over time.

[Photo source (Wikimedia Commons)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Festuca_glauca.jpg)

## Pennisetum setaceum 'Rubrum' (Purple Fountain Grass)

**Planned location:** Frontyard narrow planter/entry-walk strip, as an alternative to Dianella or Festuca.

![Pennisetum setaceum 'Rubrum' (Purple Fountain Grass)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Pennisetum%20setaceum%20Rubrum%206zz.jpg?width=800)

Features burgundy-red leaves and feathery, rosy-purple plumes.

**South Oceanside performance:** Handles Oceanside's mild winters well — frost is rare enough that it often stays evergreen or dies back only lightly — and full coastal sun brings out its best burgundy color. Two cautions for this spot: it wants a 3–4 ft spread at maturity, which will crowd a "narrow" planter and need regular shearing back; and while 'Rubrum' is a sterile cultivar (unlike the invasive green species), it's worth checking current county guidance on ornamental grasses if the planter is near any open space.

[Photo source (Wikimedia Commons)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pennisetum_setaceum_Rubrum_6zz.jpg)

## Tulbaghia violacea (Society Garlic)

**Planned location:** Backyard bed, replacing Bulbine.

![Tulbaghia violacea (Society Garlic)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Tulbaghia%20violacea%20002.JPG?width=800)

A bulbous plant with clusters of lavender-pink or purple flowers.

**South Oceanside performance:** Strong match — same low-water, full-sun/part-shade profile as the Bulbine it replaces, and its near-year-round lavender bloom suits Oceanside's mild winters (rarely cold enough to force dormancy). The garlicky scent that deters deer and rabbits is a bonus if the yard borders open space. Very low maintenance overall.

[Photo source (Wikimedia Commons)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tulbaghia_violacea_002.JPG)

## Asparagus meyeri (Foxtail Asparagus/Fern)

**Planned location:** Backyard bed, replacing Bulbine, as an alternative to Tulbaghia or Festuca.

![Asparagus meyeri (Foxtail Asparagus/Fern)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Starr_030418-0147_Asparagus_densiflorus.jpg?width=800)

Not a true fern, this plant has dense, plume-like stems of bright green, needle-like leaves.

**South Oceanside performance:** Will look its best with regular water and part shade — noticeably thirstier than the other two backyard options, so plan for supplemental irrigation rather than the low-water regime that suited the Bulbine. One flag: Asparagus densiflorus produces red berries that birds spread, and it's considered a minor invasive concern in some Southern California coastal sage scrub habitats — worth avoiding if this yard backs onto open space or coastal bluff habitat near Oceanside.

[Photo source (Wikimedia Commons)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Starr_030418-0147_Asparagus_densiflorus.jpg)

## Cordyline australis 'Atropurpurea' (Bronze Dracaena)

**Planned location:** Backyard planters on both sides of the bedroom sliding door (tall, upright accent).

![Cordyline australis 'Atropurpurea' (Bronze Dracaena)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Cordyline%20australis%20Red%20Star%200zz.jpg?width=800)

A palm-like plant with sword-shaped, deep burgundy-purple foliage.

_Photo shows a closely related red-foliage cultivar of Cordyline australis._

**South Oceanside performance:** Well suited — tolerates the wind and mild salt air typical of a coastal Oceanside lot, and its upright 6–10 ft habit reads well as a vertical accent framing a doorway. Comfortably hardy past Oceanside's mildest winter lows. Give it decent drainage in the planters and moderate water.

[Photo source (Wikimedia Commons)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cordyline_australis_Red_Star_0zz.jpg)

## Cordyline stricta

**Planned location:** Backyard planters flanking the bedroom sliding door, as an alternative to Cordyline australis.

![Cordyline stricta](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Cordyline.stricta.jpg?width=800)

An evergreen shrub with dark, glossy green, sword-like leaves.

**South Oceanside performance:** Similar upright form (narrower, 6–8 ft) and generally a good match for the mild climate, but it's more prone to leaf scorch in reflected or full all-day sun than C. australis. If those planters get strong afternoon sun off the house wall, C. australis or Cereus peruvianus would be the safer pick. It's slightly less cold-hardy, which is a non-issue in South Oceanside's near-frost-free zone.

[Photo source (Wikimedia Commons)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cordyline.stricta.jpg)

## Cereus peruvianus (Mexican Fence Post Cactus)

**Planned location:** Backyard planters flanking the bedroom sliding door, as the succulent alternative to the Cordylines.

![Cereus peruvianus (Mexican Fence Post Cactus)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Caryophyllales%20-%20Pachycereus%20marginatus%20-%201.jpg?width=800)

A tall, columnar cactus with ribbed stems and small spines.

**South Oceanside performance:** The lowest-water, lowest-maintenance of the three door-planter options — thrives in full sun and lean soil, and Oceanside's dry summers and light winter rainfall suit it well. Gives strong vertical form quickly. Main consideration for this spot is the spines near a frequently used door/walkway — keep it set back from where people brush past.

[Photo source (Wikimedia Commons)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Caryophyllales_-_Pachycereus_marginatus_-_1.jpg)
